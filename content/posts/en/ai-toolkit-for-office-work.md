Thousands of AI tools launch every year, but you only need a few that you use well. Instead of chasing the latest "hot app", start from the **work** you do every day.

## Category 1: General-purpose AI assistants

**Examples:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot.

The Swiss Army knife for writing, summarising, translating, brainstorming and explaining. For most office workers, **one assistant used fluently** beats ten tools used half-heartedly.

**Great for:** drafting emails, polishing text, summarising long documents, outlining reports.

## Category 2: Search with citations

**Examples:** Perplexity, web-search modes inside AI assistants.

When you need information that is **current and sourced**, use an AI search tool rather than a plain assistant. Always click through to verify important figures.

## Category 3: AI inside the tools you already use

**Examples:** AI in Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Notion, Canva.

The big win is **no context switching**: summarise email in your inbox, generate formulas in your spreadsheet, get design suggestions in your slides.

## Category 4: Meeting notes and summaries

Record-transcribe-summarise tools let you focus on the discussion instead of note-taking. **Important:** always inform participants and get consent before recording.

## How to choose: 4 questions

1. **How often do I do this each week?** Automate the most repetitive work first.
2. **Is the data sensitive?** For customer or financial data, use business plans with clear privacy commitments, and follow company policy.
3. **Does it integrate with my current systems?**
4. **Cost vs. time saved?** A tool that saves two hours a week usually pays for itself many times over.

## A two-week starter plan

| Week | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pick **one** general assistant and use it for every email and document |
| 2 | List your 3 most time-consuming tasks and try a specialised tool for each |

> The best tool is the one you **actually use every day**.
